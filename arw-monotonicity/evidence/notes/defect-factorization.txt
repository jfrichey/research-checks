1. DEFINITIONS AND GENERAL FACTORIZATION (PROOF)

Keep the checkpoint assumptions: finite graph, K with an absorbing sink
accessible from every vertex, 0<p_z<1, and all-active original inputs.
Let v be the target, A a target-free binary support, z in A, and

 a(A) = P[ARW from active A never visits v].

J_z forces the particle at z to take a K-jump, then performs sleep-free
surplus IDLA to restore a binary active support. Outcomes visiting v are
discarded. Sink exit removes a particle and is included, not discarded.
The known defect is e(A,z)=a(A)-sum_C J_z(A,C)a(C).

Let nu_{A\{z}}^{v,z}(B) be the SUBPROBABILITY of stabilizing active A\{z}
at sleeping B without ever visiting v or z. Do not condition and renormalize
this measure. B avoids {v,z} and |B|<=|A|-1. Define

 H_v(B,z) = P[one active at z plus sleepers at B ever visit v].

Then generally

 e(A,z) = p_z/(1-p_z) sum_B nu_{A\{z}}^{v,z}(B) H_v(B,z).       (1)

Proof. Let S(A,z) be avoidance with z initially sleeping and all other
A-particles active. Schedule z's first instruction first in the all-active
system. The sleep branch gives S; the jump branch can be legally reduced
by surplus IDLA, leaving fresh suffixes. Therefore

 a(A) = p_z S(A,z) + (1-p_z)(J_z a)(A).                       (2)

To compare S and a, hold the mark at z and explore the common particles
from A\{z}. Stop at the first visit to v or z, or stabilization. Before
that stop the exploration is identical for an active or sleeping mark.
Visiting v makes both avoidance indicators zero. Visiting z makes the two
configurations identical (two active particles there), so continuations
cancel. On stabilization avoiding both, the sleeper version is stable;
the active version releases one active z into sleeping B, losing avoidance
with probability H_v(B,z). Thus S-a=sum_B nu(B)H_v(B,z). Rearranging (2)
proves (1), hence e>=0. Finite stabilization makes the stopped exploration
exhaustive; fresh suffixes and the abelian/Markov property justify holding
z. No monotonicity for arbitrary sleeping inputs is used.

This specializes VA's recorded first-contact cancellation to visitation.
The new use retains creation weights while paying signed complete-response
coefficients. No originality claim is made.

4. GENERAL BLOCK RULE; UNPROVED SIGN QUESTION

Fix B and put W_B=V\(B union {v}). Before its first collision, target hit,
sink exit or sleep, a single active has substochastic walk kernel

 Q_B(z,y)=(1-p_z)K(z,y), z,y in W_B.

Sleep and sink exit contribute zero; target entry contributes one; entry
to y in B leads to two actives at y with the other B-particles sleeping.
Let g_B(z)>=0 be target-entry probability plus collision-entry probabilities
times their eventual target-hit probabilities. First-step conditioning gives

 H_B=(I-Q_B)^(-1)g_B,
 sum_z kappa(B,z)H_B(z)=w_B g_B,
 w_B=kappa_B(I-Q_B)^(-1).                                    (7)

This inverse always exists and is nonnegative: Q_B's row sums are at most
max_z(1-p_z)<1. This is separate from AF's signed-renewal invertibility.

Every w_B is nonnegative in the example. Only B={2} is nontrivial, since
all other kappa_B are already nonnegative. Here W_B={0,3}, Q_B(3,0)=15/32,
and w_B(0)=-b+(15/32)t>0, w_B(3)=t>0. The script also forms every (7),
computes collision credits from raw ARW, and verifies their sum plus c0
equals Delta. Exact values are in defect_factorization.json.

Candidate sufficient invariant: c0>=0 and every w_B>=0 for the full
all-active response, retaining its creation law. This is UNPROVED, stronger
than the target inequality, and could fail while the conjecture holds.
Termination of Q_B does not establish positivity of signed entrance w_B.
