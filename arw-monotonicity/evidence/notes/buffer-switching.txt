GENERAL ARW COUPLING: INITIAL SLEEP INSERTION AND BUFFER-RECORD SWITCHING
2026-10-06, Shanghai time (2026-10-05 UTC)

Status: the main single-site add-one conjecture remains unproved. This pass
focuses on general identities and local rules for a possible backward
coupling. It does not promote the preceding finite-reservoir certificates
to a general theorem. The useful new organization is: reduce the extra active
particle to an extra initial sleeper; encode histories by local buffer resets;
exchange a stored bit against an outgoing jump; permit consumed records to
move into unused continuations during global reconstruction.

The standard Abelian property and fresh-suffix resampling underpin the
reductions. They are not claimed as new results. What is new in this research
checkpoint is their use to formulate and test the particular repair rules.

1. AN EXACT FIRST-SLEEP MIXTURE ON AN ARBITRARY FINITE GRAPH

Let K be the jump kernel on finite V with an absorbing sink dagger reachable
from every vertex. Let 0<=p_y<1 be the sleep probability at y. Initial eta is
arbitrary, finite and all active. Set A=supp(eta). Write mu_eta for its final
sleeping-set law, and nu_eta,y for the final law from eta plus one INITIAL
SLEEPER at y, where y is outside A.

Define kappa_y=p_y for y outside A and kappa_y=0 for y in A. On V define

    Q_eta(y,z) = (1-kappa_y) K(y,z),
    H_eta = (I-Q_eta)^(-1) diag(kappa),
    r_eta(x) = 1 - sum_y H_eta(x,y).

Here H_eta(x,y) is the probability that a K-walk from x falls asleep at y
before reaching the sink, with sleep allowed only outside A. In particular
H_eta is nonnegative and substochastic. Its columns in A vanish.

There is an exact identity of FULL final-configuration laws:

    mu_(eta+delta_x)
      = r_eta(x) mu_eta + sum_(y outside A) H_eta(x,y) nu_eta,y.       (1)

Proof. Freeze the original eta particles in place while moving just the
additional particle. At a vertex of A there are at least two active particles,
so skip ineffective sleeps and jump one particle, leaving eta unchanged. At
a vertex outside A, the additional particle is alone; either it sleeps there
or jumps. Stop upon its first effective sleep or its exit to the sink. This
is a legal partial stabilization. The resulting configuration is either eta
or eta plus a sleeper outside A. The unused stack suffixes are independent
with their original laws, even conditional on the explored prefix. Finish
stabilizing and apply the Abelian property. This gives (1).

Consequently, for any target v, put

    D_eta(x;v) = P(v in Stab(eta+delta_x)) - P(v in Stab(eta)),
    B_eta(y;v) = P(v in Stab(eta+rho_y)) - P(v in Stab(eta)).

Then

    D_eta(x;v) = sum_(y outside A) H_eta(x,y) B_eta(y;v).             (2)

One sufficient auxiliary conjecture is B_eta(y;v)>=0 for every all-active
eta, every y outside its support, and every v. This compares an empty initial
sleeping background with a single extra sleeper; it does NOT assert
attractiveness between arbitrary sleeping backgrounds. The latter is false.
The auxiliary assertion remains unproved here and may be stronger than needed:
the original conjecture only requires the H_eta-weighted averages in (2)
to be nonnegative. Do not impose positivity on every conditioned history.

The same relation can be written as a maximum-principle equation:

    D_eta(x;v) = sum_z K(x,z) D_eta(z;v),                  x in A,
    D_eta(x;v) = p_x B_eta(x;v)
                 + (1-p_x) sum_z K(x,z) D_eta(z;v),      x outside A,

with the sink value zero. The mixture proof explains the nonnegative Green
kernel without assuming the desired conclusion.

Why this helps the backward architecture: all common active inputs remain
identical, and the only extra boundary datum is now a stored particle at one
initially vacant site. A proposed switching procedure can aim to connect the
target's terminal occupancy discrepancy to that single initial stored bit.
This is a change of boundary data, not a proof of the necessary switch.

2. LOCAL BUFFER RESETS AS A COMPLETE-HISTORY ENCODING

Process one incoming particle at a site whose local relaxed occupancy is
b in {0,1}. Excluding incoming particles not yet processed at that site,
relax it and regard outgoing jumps as pending deliveries. Its new local
occupancy c is a fresh Bernoulli(p_y) variable, independent of b. It emits

    1+b-c                                                        (3)

particles, each with an independent K(y,.) destination.

For b=0 this is the usual sleep-or-jump choice for one active particle. For
b=1 the arrival wakes the resident: first one particle must jump, then the
remaining single particle sleeps or jumps. Ineffective sleep instructions
during the forced jump have been integrated out. Delaying packet deliveries,
including self-loops, is handled through the Abelian local-input formulation;
it is not an assertion that a physically present particle may be ignored
when deciding whether a raw sleep instruction is effective.

One exact realization gives each site independent books

    B_y(1),B_y(2),... iid Bernoulli(p_y),
    J_y(1),J_y(2),... iid with law K(y,.).

Set B_y(0)=b_y, the initial buffer occupancy. After m inputs at y, the
cumulative number of emitted jumps is

    C_y(m) = m+b_y-B_y(m).                                       (4)

Its increment is 1+B_y(m-1)-B_y(m), which is always 0,1,or 2. Hence C_y
is nondecreasing. The site sends the first C_y(m) destinations from J_y.
Fixed books therefore define a deterministic unary Abelian processor: total
outputs depend only on total inputs, and any legal complete execution agrees.

Starting from all-active eta corresponds to empty buffers b=0 and pending
input counts eta. An initially sleeping extra particle sets one b_y=1.
If N_z,y(u) counts jumps to y among the first u entries of J_z, then total
inputs M_y and outputs U_y obey

    M_y = eta_y + sum_z N_z,y(U_z),
    U_y = M_y+b_y-B_y(M_y).                                      (5)

The legal execution selects the least solution. The final sleeping indicator
is B_y(M_y). These equations describe complete fields; they do not prescribe
a synchronized time evolution for the two coupled systems.

The same-book comparison increases input and output counts under an active
addition, but does not order the final buffer bits. buffer_stack_results.json
contains an explicit same-book failure. Thus the encoding does not silently
assume the original monotonicity conjecture or bypass the known obstruction.

3. AN EXACT LOCAL SWITCH: A SLEEPER FOR A JUMP

Take one specified reset record from each of two histories at the SAME site.
Suppose their old occupancies are b,c and their new occupancies are 1,0.
Their output words have lengths b and 1+c. Write them as L and R y, where
|L|=b, |R|=c, and y is the last destination of the second output word.

The following local transformation is an involution:

    first record:  b -> 1, outputs L
    second record: c -> 0, outputs R y

becomes

    first record:  b -> 0, outputs L y
    second record: c -> 1, outputs R.                            (6)

The old occupancies and incoming particles have not changed. Equation (3)
holds in both records. A stored particle moves from the first history to the
second, and one emitted particle moves in the opposite direction, with the
SAME destination y. To invert, take the final output y from the first record
and swap the new occupancy bits back.

Both sides have probability weight

    p_site (1-p_site) product_(a in L) K(site,a)
                          product_(a in R) K(site,a) K(site,y).  (7)

This requires no reversibility of K, no homogeneous sleep parameter, and no
comparison of conditional return hazards. It is an exact identity for the
specified local records. Their emitted particle is a finite-history port
whose recipient must be accounted for during a GLOBAL reconstruction.

Two cautions are essential. The new records may not yet fit into two legal
complete histories, because the transferred jump changes later arrivals.
Also, a history-adaptive choice of the records is not automatically an
invertible or measure-preserving selection rule. Equation (6) solves only
the local record and weight calculation. These are the outstanding global
issues, not details that can be omitted.

4. WHY TWO INDEPENDENT HISTORIES ARE A USEFUL ACCOUNTING SPACE

For the initially sleeping insertion problem, let q_minus and q_plus be the
two target occupation probabilities. Under independent complete histories,

    P(type 01) - P(type 10)
       = (1-q_minus)q_plus - q_minus(1-q_plus)
       = q_plus-q_minus.                                       (8)

Thus an inverse-recognizable injection of bad history pairs into good history
pairs, preserving their joint probability mass, would prove B_eta(y;v)>=0.
A suitable fractional transport would suffice too. Record exchanges need
preserve the JOINT product weight, so records can move between copies. The
separate record inventories of the two copies need not stay fixed.

For a complete buffer-history cylinder the weight is a product of reset coin
probabilities and jump probabilities. Preserving combined per-site counts of
reset outcomes and destination cards therefore preserves its weight. The
local switch (6) does exactly this. This two-copy formulation is not, by itself,
a solution to the supply/injectivity problem found in the earlier work.

5. USED RECORDS MUST BE ALLOWED TO BECOME UNUSED

A tempting further restriction is false: one cannot always repair bad pairs
using only their consumed records while preserving the combined consumed
inventory. There is already an exact two-site obstruction.

Take K(0,1)=K(0,dagger)=K(1,0)=K(1,dagger)=1/2. Start one active particle at
0, insert an initial sleeper at 1 in the upper copy, and target v=0.
Consider these independent complete histories:

  lower: the particle at 0 sleeps immediately;
  upper: the particle at 0 jumps to 1, wakes the extra sleeper, and both
         particles jump to the sink.

This is a bad pair. Its combined buffer inventory is:

  resets at 0: one 1 and one 0;
  resets at 1: one 0;
  jumps: one 0->1 and two 1->dagger.

No good pair has exactly this CONSUMED inventory. A good upper history must
leave its particle at 0 asleep. Because there is no return card 1->0, that
particle must sleep on its initial reset, leaving the upper initial sleeper
at 1 untouched. The lower particle then uses 0->1 and one 1->dagger card.
It cannot consume the second 1->dagger card: there is no second lower particle.

The natural repair is nevertheless clear. Swap the target resets; move the
0->1 jump and the zero reset at 1 to the lower history. The repaired upper
history keeps both initial particles asleep, while the lower particle exits.
One formerly used jump at 1 has become UNUSED. Retaining that card in the
unused continuation restores the exact enlarged-cylinder weight.

For arbitrary admissible parameters, the bad cylinder above has weight

    p_0 (1-p_0)(1-p_1) K(0,1) K(1,dagger)^2.

The unpadded good cylinder has weight

    p_0 (1-p_0)(1-p_1) K(0,1) K(1,dagger).

Specify one otherwise-unused good-side jump as dagger to recover the bad
weight exactly. The remaining possible values of that unused card leave
additional good mass. Algebraically, the permissible operation is the
normalization sum_z K(1,z)=1, not a claim of coefficientwise positivity
at every consumed-inventory monomial.

The same consumed-record obstruction appears for an initially ACTIVE extra
particle at 1: the upper history first sleeps that particle, then wakes it
with the arrival from 0. The extra effective sleep record is also retained
under the analogous good reconstruction.

paired_record_pools.py counts all complete histories with at most nine
combined reset records and compares the exact integer counts in each joint
inventory. The smallest sleeping-insertion obstruction has only three total
resets, and the active-addition obstruction has four. Hence these particular
obstructions are exhaustive finite counts, not truncation artifacts. The
conclusion is about a restrictive reconstruction rule, not the main ARW
conjecture.

6. THE PROPOSED GENERAL RECONSTRUCTION TARGET

The first-sleep mixture leaves one exceptional initial stored bit, with all
common active inputs paired. Start from a bad pair of completed histories,
mark the lower terminal target sleeper, and attempt the following offline
construction:

  - Use (6), or transfer a whole record if one copy never visited the site,
    to move the marked occupancy into the upper history.
  - Treat the compensating emitted particle as an unmatched incoming/outgoing
    port in the records. Follow the records responsible for the affected
    arrivals and residents, retaining their creation histories.
  - Exchange further records to match the ports. Intermediate configurations
    need not preserve the original marked success.
  - When a record ceases to be used, keep it in a marked unused continuation.
    Exposing one unused coin or jump partitions a cylinder with total weight
    one: p+(1-p)=1 or sum_z K(site,z)=1.
  - Require a decoding rule or an exact fractional ledger. A record search
    without one can again spend the same good mass multiple times.

The missing general lemma is that this process can close all its open ports
with a recognizable inverse (or a globally balanced fractional flow), with
almost-sure termination and no negative boundary contribution. The intended
favorable boundary is the single extra initial sleeper. Matched common
inputs should cancel in pairs; visits to the sink and loops must be handled
explicitly. None of these closure assertions is proved here.

This target is more concrete than demanding weighted expansion without an
ARW mechanism: the two local probability-preserving operations are now
specified, namely record switching (6) and revealing/unreading independent
continuation cards. Their composition still requires the global proof.
The finite pair example explains why a rule that forbids unreading is
incorrect even before questions of efficiency arise.

7. COMPUTATIONAL CHECKS AND THEIR SCOPE

general_balance.py compares initially sleeping insertion with the all-active
baseline on 36 path/directed-kernel cases with 2--5 sites, including unequal
sleep probabilities. No negative single-site difference exceeded roundoff.
It also probes increasing sleeping backgrounds around a single active target;
that diagnostic is not used as a theorem. bottom_background_results.json
checks arbitrary additional disjoint sleeping sets in the same cases, again
finding no beyond-roundoff negative difference. These are finite numerical
checks of stronger candidate inequalities, not proofs or exhaustive searches
over parameters.

buffer_stack.py checks schedule independence and increasing input/output
counts on 500 fixed-book samples, and saves a final-bit order obstruction.

verify_general_balance.py independently solves the original raw-instruction
absorbing chain and the buffer/pending-input chain, comparing the FULL final
configuration distributions on all 0/active/sleeping starts for three small
kernels, including a directed kernel with self-loops and unequal rates. The
maximum discrepancy is 2.23e-16. It also checks the full-law identity (1),
with maximum discrepancy 3.34e-16. The general proofs are the arguments above;
these checks audit the implementations and the self-loop/delay conventions.

paired_record_pools.py uses integer counting, not floating-point flow, for
the consumed-inventory obstruction in Section 5.

Run (Python 3, NumPy and SciPy):

    python general_balance.py
    python buffer_stack.py
    python verify_general_balance.py
    python paired_record_pools.py

Primary outputs: general_balance_verification.json,
paired_record_pool_results.json, general_balance_results.json,
buffer_stack_results.json, bottom_background_results.json.
