Question and stopping criterion
-------------------------------
Construct the first-contact stopping-set rule requested by MONOTONICITY.md.
Check whether its endpoint law conditional on the discovered block B is the
fixed-block kernel W_(S,B). If not, identify the exact correction. Then test
whether its properly normalized random-block version repairs the specific
heterogeneous obstruction from the preceding chunk. Stop at this decision.

Only VA Sections 2-4 and the preceding LD certificate are needed. No old
finite audits were rerun. The positive construction below is a disintegration
of VA's existing deferred-mark sampler, not a new monotonicity theorem.

1. A precise first-contact rule
-------------------------------
Write U=V\S and R for the final odds, r^S for odds R on S and zero on U.
Let C be the present sleeping support. The law of completing all remaining
activations is T_(r^S,R)(C,.): wake every current sleeper on U, leave the
sleepers on S asleep, and stabilize at the final parameters.

Keep a dormant mark at each sleeper on U. Select any x in U before revealing
a new avalanche. Start B={x}; erase the mark at x and wake its sleeper if
present. Run the avalanche at FINAL sleep probabilities. Whenever a jump
first reaches y in U\B, put y in B before toppling there; erase its mark if
present. An arrival at its sleeper has already made two active particles.
Also include visited sites that were initially empty. End when this seed
avalanche has no active particles. Let D be the resulting sleeping support.

The sites outside S union B have never been touched; their sleepers are
exactly the original dormant marked particles from C. Return (S union B,D).
Repeat with another unprocessed seed if desired. Empty seeds simply add a
vertex to B and do nothing else. Thus there are at most |V| seed avalanches.

Why this is legal: in the mixed initial configuration defining T, regard
unexamined marks as active particles that are deliberately not toppled yet.
Run only the seed and particles released by contact. On arrival at a mark,
an initially active held particle and a sleeper produce the same two-active
state. At visited empty sites, use the final sleep probability immediately;
this is an essential difference from the old singleton parameter update.
Once an avalanche ends, the remaining held particles are precisely the
unvisited marks. All particles are eventually released, so the Abelian
property gives the intended completed law. Conditional unused instructions
retain their laws, because this is a legal stopped exploration.

This proof works for every partial state (S,C); it does not assert that
arbitrary pairs of such sleeping supports have monotone completion laws.

2. Correct positive kernels and normalization (PROOF)
----------------------------------------------------
Define the subprobability kernel

  L_(S,x)^B(C,D) = P(first-contact rule returns block B and support D).

Each B is a subset of U containing x. These kernels satisfy

  sum_(B,D) L_(S,x)^B(C,D) = 1,

  sum_B L_(S,x)^B T_(r^(S union B),R) = T_(r^S,R).       (FC)

The second formula is an identity of complete endpoint laws. Continue each
stopped exploration by releasing the remaining marks; the preceding legal
schedule proves it. It also proves that H(S,C)=T_(r^S,R)h(C) is a martingale
for this random-block chain. Seeds may be chosen using both copies' past
provided the conditional marginal law of (B,D) is the complete joint L law.

There is a useful event-level interpretation of each L term. For a FIXED B,
sample W_(S,B) using a legal schedule that initially holds all activated
particles in B other than x. Run the seed until it dies, releasing held
particles only when contacted. Let E_B be the event that this exploration
visits every vertex of B\{x} and never reaches U\B. On E_B every held particle
has been released and the system has already stabilized. On the complement,
finish the ordinary fixed-block stabilization with any legal schedule.
The paths contributing to E_B agree exactly with the adaptive discovery
of B. Therefore

  L_(S,x)^B(C,D) = P_W(E_B and endpoint D)
                = W_(S,B)(C,D) P_W(E_B | endpoint D).

The equality uses the chosen legal schedule to define E_B. It is not an
assumption that E_B is independent of the endpoint. In particular, L<=W
entrywise, but L is usually substochastic. Dividing by P(E_B) generally
produces a biased version of W. The selection event and its mass must be
retained. This supplies an exact positive mass ledger without unreading
sleep instructions or making F3/F6's unsupported positivity assumptions.

3. Minimal conditioning obstruction (PROOF; also checked exactly)
----------------------------------------------------------------
Take 0->1->sink, p_0=p_1=1/2, S empty, C={0,1}, seed 0.
The first-contact joint law is

  B={0},   D={0,1}: probability 1/2;
  B={0,1}, D={1}:   probability 1/4;
  B={0,1}, D=empty: probability 1/4.

Indeed, the seed either sleeps immediately or jumps to 1. In the latter
case two active particles at 1 send one to the sink; the last particle
sleeps or exits with equal probabilities.

Conditioning on B={0} gives D={0,1} with probability one. In contrast, the
fixed kernel W_(empty,{0}) leaves site 1 at infinite sleep rate, giving
D={0,1} and D={1} with probability 1/2 each. Thus conditioning on discovered
B does NOT recover the prescribed W law, already on two sites with a
legitimate binary all-active starting input.

The correction (FC) is exact: the no-contact branch subsequently activates
site 1, yielding {0,1} and {0}, each with total weight 1/4. Together with the
contact branch, all four final supports have probability 1/4, as direct
ordinary all-active stabilization also gives.

4. The valid random-block rule still fails the coupling test (COMPUTATION)
------------------------------------------------------------------------
Use LD's new heterogeneous example:

  0 -> 1,2,sink with probabilities 1/4,1/4,1/2;
  1 -> 2,3 with probabilities 1/2,1/2;
  2 -> 2,3 with probabilities 1/2,1/2;
  3 -> 0,3 with probabilities 1/2,1/2;
  p=(1/2,3/5,1/2,1/2).

At S={1,3}, C={0,1,2}, D={0,1,2,3}, target 0, let each copy choose seed
0 or 2 and reveal its complete first-contact avalanche, including its
random discovered block. Blocks can differ between the copies.

For each resulting (B,E), compute the conditional final target probability
H(S union B,E). An almost-surely target-monotone continuation requires the
lower H distribution to be stochastically dominated by the upper one.
The following exact upper-tail deficits rule out every seed pair:

All deficits are strictly positive. This includes arbitrary couplings of
the two complete first-contact outputs, different seeds, and arbitrary
valid conditional completions afterwards. Randomizing seed choices cannot
restore probability-one success. The proof is the conditional-expectation
argument from LD, now applied to the joint random-block kernels L.

This does NOT contradict the positive simultaneous {0,2} target gap.
The simultaneous block permits coupling complete continuations across the
first-contact outcomes; it does not require the two first-contact outputs
to be revealed as a step whose subsequent conditional marginal completions
remain unchanged. Nor does this rule out avoiding this state with another
initial policy. No general counterexample is claimed.

The exact script solves absorption for states (active/sleeping counts,B),
using final p at every active site and recording every newly visited site.
It checks unit mass and nonnegativity, and verifies (FC) as an identity of
full terminal distributions in all five cases: two seeds times two supports
on the four-site witness, plus the two-site example. The JSON retains all
joint output masses and posterior values, so every tail is reproducible.

5. What changed and one next task
---------------------------------
The selection-law question is now resolved for this particular rule:
conditioning on B fails, but keeping its positive subprobability law gives
a valid endpoint sampler. The coupling test decisively rejects closing a
step at the end of one contact-discovered avalanche in the saved state.

Next: use the two-seed decomposition

  T_(r^S,R) = L_(S,x)^{ {x,y} }
             + L_(S,x)^{ {x} } W_(S union {x},{y})

when U={x,y}. Expand the TARGET increment for the LD witness into contact
and no-contact complete histories. Seek one explicit probability-preserving
exchange between these two classes that repairs the posterior deficit while
retaining both full endpoint laws. Its normalization must use the L masses;
a terminal max-flow alone is not the deliverable. If only a quantitative
mass obstruction is found, record that. This moves the search to whole
continuations across contact histories before committing their outcomes.
