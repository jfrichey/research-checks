1. SOURCE AND BLOCK DEFINITIONS

Preserve the checkpoint assumptions: finite graph, accessible absorbing
sink from every vertex, 0<p_z<1, original inputs all active. Let v be target.
At common-particle bound two, NC supplies an exact full-response identity

 Delta=C+sum_c d(c)G(c),                                    (1)

where G(c) is probability of ever visiting v from two-active configuration
c, C is a constant collecting earlier target hits, and d is supported on
collision configurations C_z=2 delta_z. It may have negative entries.
The occupation increment Delta itself is not a visitation probability.

For a site-priority order pi, run raw ARW on two-active configurations,
stopping on an effective sleep, a particle's exit to the sink, or visiting
v. Q_pi is the kernel between states before stopping; R_pi is its kernel
to stopping states. Ineffective sleep instructions at a collision are
self-loops. The signed visit and boundary vectors are

 t_pi=d(I-Q_pi)^(-1),       beta_pi=t_pi R_pi.               (2)

The inverse exists: this is a finite stopped ARW phase under the original
assumptions. Removing ineffective sleep self-loops rescales corresponding
visit weights by positive factors and leaves beta unchanged.

By the Markov property, (1) also equals

 Delta=C+sum_b beta_pi(b)H_v(b),                            (3)

where H_v(b)=1 for a boundary state already visiting v, and otherwise is
its probability of a future visit. This identity is valid for either sign
of the coefficients. Abelian final-law invariance does not assert that
intermediate block coefficient vectors have a schedule-independent sign.

2. GENERAL PRIORITY-SITE OBSTRUCTION (PROOF)

Suppose z!=v has highest priority, and write C_z=2 delta_z. No distinct
two-active configuration can transition to C_z:

* From two particles together elsewhere, one jump leaves their old site
  occupied, so it cannot put both at z.
* From particles at two different sites, producing C_z requires moving the
  OTHER particle while one is already at z. The priority rule moves z first.

Thus the only entry to C_z in the phase comes from its initial signed
source d(C_z) and its own self-loops. Those self-loops have probability
p_z+(1-p_z)K(z,z). The balance equation gives

 t_pi(C_z)=d(C_z)/[(1-p_z)(1-K(z,z))].                      (4)

Sink accessibility ensures K(z,z)<1. In particular, a negative d(C_z)
cannot be repaired by any amount of positive source at other states under
this priority rule.

The same obstruction reaches a complete stopping outcome. Let e_z denote
one active particle at z and nothing else. The only transition exiting
this phase at e_z is a sink jump from C_z. In a split state containing z,
priority makes z move, so a sink jump leaves the OTHER site occupied.
Therefore

 beta_pi(e_z)=d(C_z)K(z,sink)/(1-K(z,z)).                   (5)

If d(C_z)<0 and K(z,sink)>0, both (4) and (5) are negative. Consequently
replacing visit-weight positivity by positivity only of complete stopping
outcomes does not rescue the proposed rule. This is a general structural
statement about this signed-source representation and scheduler.

4. CONSEQUENCE FOR THE PROOF ARCHITECTURE

This is new F11. F10 was an obstruction to stopping at first collision;
F11 shows that a longer block with an arbitrary fixed priority can still
fail, even at its complete stopping outcomes. NC's successful transfer
held the receiving particle still so another particle could join it.
Giving the receiving vertex highest priority prevents exactly that move.

This does not rule out source-dependent scheduling, randomized scheduling,
or cancellations involving later phases. It provides no theorem that such
schemes have enough capacity. The observed success for the original order
is not promoted to a general rule. Automatic block enlargement and further
fixed-order sweeps are parked until there is an additional balance argument.

5. A SPECIFIC NEXT BALANCE QUESTION (UNPROVED)

For a source with one negative collision coefficient d(C_z)=-b, define a
debt-directed policy: once one particle reaches z, hold it and move the
other. Before reaching z use a fixed legal order. Stop at C_z or at the
first effective sleep, sink loss or target visit. Let h_z(c) be the
probability of reaching C_z first from a positive-source state c.

First-step/strong Markov conditioning gives the exact general identity

 sum_c d(c)G(c)
  = [sum_{d(c)>0} d(c)h_z(c)-b] G(C_z)
    + sum_{d(c)>0} d(c) E_c[H_v(X_tau); X_tau!=C_z].          (6)

The second term is nonnegative. Hence the concrete sufficient capacity
inequality is

 sum_{d(c)>0} d(c)h_z(c) >= b.                              (7)

Next chunk: derive h_z from the held-particle walk and test/prove or refute
(7) for the actual all-active creation coefficients, first at common bound
two with one negative collision source. This capacity question is stronger
than the occupation inequality: it must cancel at C_z before phase exit.
It does not assume the desired target increment inside a transport fit.
No universal validity is claimed. Multiple negative collision sources,
larger avalanches, and general signed-renewal invertibility remain open.
