1. GENERAL MEETING BOUND (PROOF)

Keep the checkpoint assumptions: finite graph, sink accessible from every
vertex, 0<p_x<1, and all-active original inputs. Fix distinct target v and
meeting vertex z. Let g_z(x) be the probability that ONE active particle
from x reaches z before sleeping, hitting v or exiting to the sink. Set
g_z(z)=1, g_z(v)=g_z(sink)=0. For x outside {v,z},

 g_z(x)=(1-p_x) sum_y K(x,y)g_z(y).                         (1)

These values come from a killed-walk inverse on V\{v,z}, whose row sums
are bounded by max_x(1-p_x)<1.

Run TWO active particles under any causal legal scheduler, stopping when
both reach z or at the first effective sleep, sink loss or visit to v.
Before stopping put F(x,y)=min(g_z(x),g_z(y)); give the successful boundary
value 1 and all failure boundaries value 0. This is a supermartingale for
EVERY choice of legal toppling:

* At distinct x,y, suppose x is toppled. If g_z(x)<=g_z(y), expected next
  F is at most (1-p_x)sum K(x,w)g_z(w)=g_z(x). Here x!=z.
  If g_z(x)>g_z(y), it is at most (1-p_x)g_z(y)<=g_z(y).
* With two actives together at x, a mandatory jump produces x,w, whose
  minimum is at most g_z(x). Ineffective sleep self-loops preserve F.

Boundary failures have value zero, so they respect these inequalities.
Optional stopping (or bounded finite-time stopping followed by a limit)
therefore proves

 P_{x,y}(both reach z before phase failure)<=min(g_z(x),g_z(y)). (2)

This holds for randomized, history-dependent causal schedules. It does
not constrain anticipative transformations of instruction fields.

Once one particle is at z, holding it and moving the other ATTAINS (2):
the remaining success probability is exactly g_z(y). Holding the first
arrival is thus optimal at that stage, although the pre-arrival scheduling
can matter. For a collision C_y=2 delta_y, the universal upper bound is
g_z(y), not necessarily the exact optimal meeting probability.

2. WHY THIS TESTS THE PROPOSED CANCELLATION

NC/PR express a complete occupation increment as

 Delta=C+sum_y d_y G_y,                                    (3)

where G_y is probability of ever hitting v from two actives at y. Assume
the only negative collision coefficient is d_z=-b. Meeting-based repair
requires sum_{d_y>0} d_y h_z(C_y)>=b, where h is meeting probability before
phase failure. By (2), a NECESSARY condition for ANY such causal repair is

 sum_{d_y>0} d_y g_z(y)>=b.                                (4)

The constant C and successful target-hit exits are excluded by this
particular repair requirement. This exclusion is a substantive restriction.

4. SCOPE, NEXT STEP, AND FAILURE RECORD

New F12 retires cancellation solely by collecting collision debt before
the first effective sleep, loss or target hit. Unlike F11, this is not a
fixed-order defect; (2) bounds all causal schedules. It leaves open budgets
that use target successes, boundary histories after sleeps/losses, several
phases, or full instruction-field transformations. F1-F11 were checked;
there was no return to arbitrary-sleeper attractiveness, separate renewal
generations, or finite transport fitting.

Next concrete question: include successful target hits in the balance.
For one debt -bG_z and C>=0, stop each positive-source history at C_z,
target v, or first sleep/loss. Let r_z(c) be probability of the first two
outcomes. The sufficient TOTAL credit condition is

 C+sum_{d(c)>0} d(c)r_z(c)>=b.                              (5)

To see this, let m be weighted meeting probability and a weighted target-hit
probability. Stopping gives (C+a)+(m-b)G_z plus nonnegative exit credits.
If m<b, use G_z<=1; if m>=b, every term is already nonnegative. Thus (5)
with C>=0 suffices. This is stronger than the desired occupation inequality
and does not assume it. Its general validity is UNPROVED.

Derive or refute (5) from the creation coefficients, rather than selecting
coefficients using already computed G values. No more meet-only scheduler
searches. A finite repair does not constitute a general balance law.
