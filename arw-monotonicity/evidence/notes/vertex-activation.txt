ARW MONOTONICITY: POSITIVE BLOCKS, ADAPTIVE VERTEX ACTIVATION,
AND AN EXACT COUPLING WITH TWO-VERTEX BLOCKS
Continuation of COMPLETE_BLOCKS.txt, 2026-10-06 UTC

STATUS / PRINCIPAL DEVELOPMENT

The main add-one conjecture remains open. The complete-block inverse now
gives a positive probabilistic construction on every finite graph:

  Start with the binary input particles asleep. Turn on vertices, in any
  adapted order, from infinite sleep rate to their desired sleep rates.
  When turning on a vertex, wake its current sleeper, if present, and finish
  the resulting avalanche. After every vertex has been turned on, the FULL
  terminal law is ordinary ARW from the original all-active input.

Several vertices may be turned on in one block. Their current sleepers are
then activated together. There are at most |V| nonempty activation blocks.
The order and blocks may depend on the already revealed joint history of
two copies, provided each selected block has its stated conditional law.

This settles marginal laws and termination for a new coupling architecture
based on complete avalanches. It does not settle how to couple the blocks
so that the final target indicators are ordered on an arbitrary graph.

On the four-vertex diagnostic graph, an exact finite-horizon search cannot
always couple one vertex activation per copy per round, even when the two
orders can differ. Allowing common blocks of up to two vertices succeeds
for all 32 binary add-one edges and all four targets. A saved coupling is
replayed to verify BOTH full original terminal marginals and zero bad mass.
There is no assertion that blocks of size two suffice on general graphs.

1. A POSITIVE INTERPRETATION OF INVERSE TIMES FORWARD STABILIZATION

Use sleep odds r_z=(1-p_z)/p_z, and write P_r for the all-active binary
stabilization matrix and E_r=P_r^{-1} from COMPLETE_BLOCKS.txt. Let
0<=r_z<=s_z. Define

    T_(r,s) = E_r P_s.                                         (1)

This is a STOCHASTIC matrix. Its row from sleeping support C has the
following law:

    independently activate the sleeper at z in C with probability
        alpha_z = 1-r_z/s_z;
    then stabilize at sleep probability p_z=1/(1+s_z).          (2)

When r_z=s_z=0, take alpha_z=0. Zero odds are interpreted by the local
buffer limit: a lone active particle sleeps immediately, whereas surplus
particles still make their mandatory K-jumps. This is the well-defined
infinite-sleep-rate limit, rather than a literal iid stack consisting only
of sleep instructions.

Proof: work in the commuting addition algebra at parameters s. Let a_z
add an active particle, j_z=sum_y K(z,y)a_y, and

    b_z(u)=(1+u_z)a_z-u_z j_z.

For s_z>0 there is the POSITIVE identity

    b_z(r) = (r_z/s_z)b_z(s) + (1-r_z/s_z)a_z.                 (3)

Applying prod_{z in C} b_z(r) to the empty state yields row C of E_r P_s.
Expand (3). For a selected subset of active sites, apply all the b_z(s)
factors first: they create precisely the other initial sleepers. Then add
the selected active particles. Commutation identifies this with the mixed
initial state in (2), and the product coefficients are its independent
activation probabilities. The argument also works at zero odds using the
buffer-limit definition; no division by zero is required at unchanged sites.

Consequently, for r<=s<=t,

    P_r T_(r,s)=P_s,
    T_(r,s) T_(s,t)=T_(r,t).                                  (4)

All rows in these identities are complete probability distributions. The
signed terms from E_r have already been cancelled, not optimized by an LP.
This does not make E_r itself nonnegative.

2. A SIGN-REVERSING FIRST-CONTACT CANCELLATION

Set e_z=a_z-b_z at fixed final parameters. The collision relation gives

    a_z e_z=0.                                                (5)

There is a direct complete-history implementation. Let S be a nonempty set
of marked initial sites, disjoint from the common initial configuration eta.
Consider the signed activation contrast

    C(eta,S) = sum_{T subset S} (-1)^(|S|-|T|)
       Law[Stab(eta + active particles on T + sleepers on S\T)]. (6)

Hold all marked particles in place. Run the common particles until they
first reach S or stabilize without visiting S. This common exploration does
not depend on the marked activation choices.

If the first hit is at z in S, toggle just the initial active/sleeping label
at z. Immediately after that arrival, both versions have two active
particles there. Their other states coincide. Complete them with identical
remaining stacks. The common prefix and the canonical first hit are unchanged
by the toggle, so toggling again is the inverse. The two signed histories
have equal probability weights and opposite signs. Every hit history cancels.

Only no-hit histories remain. Let nu_eta^S(B) be the subprobability law of
the common terminal sleeping support B together with avoidance of S. Then
the exact identity of signed FULL terminal laws is

    C(eta,S) = sum_B nu_eta^S(B) C(B sleeping,S).               (7)

This is a genuine disjoint sign-reversing cancellation within expansion
(6). It is not yet the global injection from bad add-one pairs to good pairs.
In particular, the right side of (7) is still signed. The distribution that
created B must remain attached; one cannot replace it by an arbitrary
sleeping background and assert an order there.

3. AN EXECUTABLE DEFERRED-ACTIVATION BLOCK

Formula (2) can be sampled without first revealing all activation coins.

  * Keep a mark at every original sleeper whose activation is unexamined.
  * Select a remaining mark z and toss its activation coin.
  * If inactive, erase this mark and keep the sleeper.
  * If active, wake the sleeper and complete that avalanche. Erase all
    remaining marks visited during the avalanche, without tossing their coins.
  * Repeat until no marks remain.

At a visited mark, an initially active particle held there and an initially
sleeping particle are identical after the arrival. Its unexamined coin has
total weight alpha_z+(1-alpha_z)=1 and can be left unused. This is the
specific normalization that allows used/unused boundary data to change
without inventing extra mass. All marks left unvisited still correspond to
their original sleepers. Their coins remain independent and unrevealed.

At most |C| coins are examined and at most |C| seed avalanches run. A Fraction
calculation of this recursive sampler agrees with (2) and (1), for every
input support in a directed three-vertex heterogeneous example and three
different parameter blocks. This validates the complete output distribution.

4. ADAPTIVE VERTEX ACTIVATION: A GENERAL ENDPOINT SAMPLER

Fix the final odds R. For a processed set S define r^S_z=R_z on S and zero
elsewhere. Write P_S=P_(r^S). For a nonempty batch B outside S define

    W_(S,B)=T_(r^S,r^(S union B)).                             (8)

The activation probabilities in (2) are one on B and zero elsewhere. Hence
W_(S,B) wakes every current sleeper in B and then stabilizes at the new
partial parameters. In particular, a vertex initially empty may now contain
a sleeper when its turn arrives; wake that CURRENT sleeper.

The fundamental identity is

    P_S W_(S,B)=P_(S union B),   P_empty=I.                    (9)

Start at the sleeping support A, with S empty. Choose any nonempty batch
B of unprocessed vertices, apply W_(S,B), and set S:=S union B. Stop when
S=V. The terminal law is P_V(A,.), the desired original all-active law.

Why history-dependent choices are allowed: fix a terminal test function h
and set

    H(S,C) = (T_(r^S,R) h)(C).

For EVERY permitted batch B, equation (4) gives

    W_(S,B) H(S union B,.) = H(S,.).                          (10)

Thus H(S,current support) is a bounded martingale under any adapted choice
of batches. After at most |V| steps, H=h at the terminal support. Its initial
expectation is P_V h(A), proving the entire marginal law. The batch choice
may depend on the already revealed histories of BOTH copies: (10) holds
pointwise for each selected action. The conditional law of each marginal
block, given that joint history and action, must still be W_(S,B).

Choosing a block AFTER inspecting several candidate block outcomes and
keeping a favorable one is not justified by this theorem. Either commit
using the current revealed history, or supply a separate exact conditional
resampling argument.

For arbitrary all-active multiplicities, first perform legal surplus IDLA
to obtain a binary support, retaining fresh stack suffixes. The same endpoint
sampler then applies. For add-one comparisons, the initial IDLA supports can
be coupled by inclusion, so binary add-one inputs remain the central case.

5. A FINITE WHOLE-BLOCK COUPLING FRAMEWORK

The common-parameter version has state (S,C_minus,C_plus). At each stage:

  choose a batch B subset V\S;
  couple W_(S,B)(C_minus,.) and W_(S,B)(C_plus,.);
  retain their two complete outcomes and set S:=S union B.

Neither intermediate support inclusion nor intermediate target order is
required. Only the terminal target indicators must be ordered. A finite
backward recursion can identify winning states: an action works if there is
a transport of its two exact marginal laws supported on winning next states.
This is an exact bipartite max-flow problem, with rational capacities in the
saved examples. Randomizing among losing actions cannot give success one.

The marginals and finite number of blocks are now justified independently
of the desired monotonicity. What is missing is an ARW-specific invariant
and a block/transport selection rule that works from every

    (empty, A, A union {x}).                                  (11)

Allowing the entire remaining vertex set as one batch always makes this
formulation as hard as the original terminal inequality. Therefore merely
restating the max-flow feasibility problem is not a proof. The next useful
step is to identify which creation histories or collision components require
joint treatment and prove that their transports leave a suitable invariant.

6. EXACT COUPLING AND AN EXPLICIT NEED TO GROUP VERTICES

Use the four-vertex graph from COMPLETE_BLOCKS.txt, with p=1/2 everywhere:

    0 -> 1,2,dagger with probabilities 1/4,1/4,1/2;
    1 -> 2,3 with probabilities 1/2,1/2;
    2 -> 2,3 with probabilities 1/2,1/2;
    3 -> 0,3 with probabilities 1/2,1/2.

Consider common input A={0,1,2}, extra x=3, target v=0. The saved coupling
first activates vertex 3. In certain later branches S={1,3} has been
processed, with lower support C={0,1,2} and upper D=V. The remaining vertices
are 0 and 2. Revealing either one separately can create incompatible
conditional occupation probabilities, even though completing both together
has the correct order.

For example, if both copies activate 2 next, let Z_minus,Z_plus be their
conditional probabilities of final target occupation after this revelation,
with vertex 0 still to be processed. At threshold t=32922/61243,

    P(Z_minus >= t)=7321/7327,
    P(Z_plus  >= t)=14615/14654.

The lower tail exceeds the upper by 27/14654>0. Any coupling that subsequently
guarantees target order must have Z_minus<=Z_plus almost surely, which these
tails rule out. The saved witness checks all FOUR choices of the next lower
and upper vertex (0 or 2); every choice has an exact positive tail deficit.

Activating {0,2} together instead gives target probabilities

    lower: 37534390931/63854401520,
    upper: 1106034765547/1744023341515,

whose difference is 258805482381/5580874692848>0. A coupling of that completed
block can therefore order the target. This is a precise instance in which
conditioning halfway through the needed cancellation is the obstruction.

The full saved adaptive coupling has 41 nonterminal policy rows and 145
reachable states. A separate replay checks every row's two marginal laws,
propagates all mass to the terminal states, and compares with independently
computed ordinary ARW terminal laws from the original inputs. It finds

    P(lower target=1, upper target=0)=0,
    P(lower target=0, upper target=1)
       =302240830349/13952186732120.

The expected number of two-vertex blocks in this particular coupling is
110/273. All computations here use exact Fractions, not LP tolerances.

The search with common batches of size at most two succeeds on ALL 128
binary add-one/target comparisons on this four-vertex graph. One-vertex
blocks already work for all comparisons in the separate two- and
three-vertex diagnostics. No general bound on block size is claimed.

The failed search class is specifically one vertex per copy per round,
with arbitrary adapted choices of vertex order and complete-avalanche
couplings. It includes different orders in the two copies. It does not
exhaust every asynchronous scheme or a coupling that uses unrevealed future
blocks in a separately justified noncausal construction.

7. AN IMPORTANT LIMITATION OF THE POSITIVE BLOCK KERNEL

T_(r,s) need not preserve occupancy order on arbitrary sleeping supports.
On 1 -> 0 -> dagger, use final p=1/2 and activation probability 1/2 at
each initial sleeper. Starting from {0}, the terminal occupation of 0 is
3/4. Starting from {0,1}, it is 11/16, smaller by 1/16.

Thus the parameter-change kernel is not an attractive update by itself.
The larger proof must retain the law that produced the current supports,
the processed parameter set, and sufficient information for block selection.
The endpoint sampler and the first-contact cancellation supply this framework;
they do not imply the missing general invariant automatically.

8. CONTINUOUS PARAMETER FORM (OPTIONAL, NOT NEEDED FOR THE COUPLING SEARCH)

Along r_z(t)=t R_z, P(t) solves P'(t)=P(t)Q(t), where

    Q(t)(C,.) = sum_{z in C} [W_z(t)(C,.)-delta_C]/t
              = sum_{z in C} R_z/(1+t R_z)
                    [F_z(t)(C,.)-delta_C].                   (12)

Here W_z(t) wakes one current sleeper and stabilizes at parameters tR;
F_z(t) instead forces its first jump and then stabilizes. The second form
is nonsingular at t=0 by the infinite-sleep limit. It is a genuine Markov
generator. The expected number of proposed forced-avalanche events up to
T is at most sum_z log(1+T R_z), using site clocks irrespective of occupancy.

This gives another positive complete-avalanche representation. Its individual
updates are not attractive, as Section 7 already warns. The alternative
left derivative -P E' can even have negative off-diagonal entries; exploratory
values are retained in block_parameter_flow_results.json. Neither derivative
supplies a shortcut to the main monotonicity proof.
