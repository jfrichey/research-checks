ARW MONOTONICITY: POSITIVE FLOW USING AVOIDANCE AND FORCED JUMPS
2026-10-06 UTC; continuation of RECONSTRUCTION.txt

STATUS AND RESTART POINT

The general add-one conjecture remains unproved. This pass finds a concrete
way to group the signed terms from the preceding renewal calculation. An
actual avoidance probability is superharmonic under a forced particle jump.
The previous test used only its decrease under adding particles, which lost
this constraint. In the new tests, nonnegative combinations of avoidance
probabilities and forced-jump defects account for every response row examined.
One genuinely signed four-site identity is verified exactly, independently
from raw-instruction transitions.

The next target is an arbitrary-graph positive-flow lemma in Section 4.
It concerns the original one-site occupation increment, at a specified target
sleep rate. It does not prove the stronger ordering of whole chance-count laws.
The numerical certificates are not a proof of the positive-flow lemma.

1. THE MISSING CONSTRAINT IS DYNAMICAL

Keep the finite vertex set V, accessible sink dagger, jump kernel K and
0 <= p_z < 1. For a fixed target v let

    a_v(eta) = P_eta(no particle ever visits v),

starting from all-active eta. A visit at time zero counts. Thus a_v(0)=1,
and a_v(eta)=0 whenever eta(v)>0. For binary eta=1_A abbreviate a_v(A).

For any occupied z != v, force one particle at z to jump once before running
ordinary stabilization. All other particles are held fixed during this jump.
Then

    a_v(eta) >= sum_w K(z,w) a_v(eta-delta_z+delta_w),          (1)

where delta_dagger=0.

Proof. On the original stack field, topple z through its first jump, waking
its particle whenever necessary. Follow by legal stabilization with the
remaining stacks. This is an acceptable stabilizing toppling sequence for
the original input. Least action bounds the original legal odometer by this
acceptable odometer. Therefore every originally used jump is also used by
the modified execution, so the original visited set is included in the new
one. Conditional on the first jump destination, unused stack suffixes are
fresh. The modified law after that jump is the all-active law appearing on
the right of (1). This proves the inequality for arbitrary finite K, including
directed edges and self-loops. One can equivalently replace all initial sleep
cards preceding the first jump at z by neutral instructions.

This uses standard acceptable-toppling/least-action facts; it is not presented
as a new ARW monotonicity theorem. A reference for that framework is Rolla,
Activated Random Walks on Z^d, Probability Surveys 17 (2020), Section 2.2,
especially Lemma 2.1 and equation (2.3):
https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.05037
The argument above spells out the finite-graph application needed here.

After the forced jump, eliminate multiplicities by legal sleep-free IDLA:
move surplus particles until every remaining site has at most one active
particle. This retains an active particle at any visited site, so any visit
to v is still detectable in the resulting support. It gives a substochastic
kernel J_z(A,B) on supports B not containing v. The missing mass corresponds
to visiting v, whose avoidance value is zero. A jump to dagger can instead
reduce the common particle count, and is included through the smaller support
B; it is not the same as visiting v.

Define the forced-jump defect

    e_v(A,z) = a_v(A) - sum_B J_z(A,B) a_v(B) >= 0.            (2)

Under the coupling in the proof of (1), e_v(A,z) is exactly the probability
that ordinary stabilization avoids v but the forced-jump execution visits v.
It therefore already groups complete histories into a nonnegative event.
There is no claim that final sleeping indicators are ordered by this coupling.

2. HOW THIS FITS THE PRECEDING SIGNED RENEWAL

For binary common support A and x outside A put

    D_A(x;v) = F_v(1_A+delta_x)-F_v(1_A).

The preceding checkpoint gives, after unreading sleepers, IDLA reduction and
source harmonicity, the finite exact identity

    D = M D + G_v a_v.                                      (3)

Rows are support edges (A,x), with 1 <= |A| <= N and x outside A.
The reward entry is

    (G_v a_v)_(A,x) = H_A(x,v) a_v(A),

where H_A is the first-sleep kernel with sleep suppressed on A. H_A(x,v)=0
when v is in A. The matrix M is genuinely signed. Existing negative entries,
including -8/2025 on the four-site path, remain negative: this continuation
does not claim to turn M itself into a Markov kernel.

In all computed cases I-M is invertible. Define

    L_v = (I-M)^(-1) G_v,
    D_A(x;v) = sum_B L_v((A,x),B) a_v(B).                    (4)

The coefficients of L_v can also be negative, even when v is outside A.
Thus merely summing all renewals does not supply coefficientwise positivity.
The new observation is that these signed rows admit the decomposition

    L_v((A,x),.) = c(.)
        + sum_(B,z in B) d(B,z) [delta_B(.)-J_z(B,.)],       (5)

with c >= 0 and d >= 0, in the tested cases. Combining (2), (4), (5) gives

    D_A(x;v) = sum_B c(B) a_v(B) + sum_(B,z) d(B,z)e_v(B,z)
               >= 0.                                      (6)

The coefficients may depend on K, p, v, A and x. Equation (5) is stronger
than positivity at the actual ARW avoidance vector: it guarantees positivity
for every nonnegative vector superharmonic under all these forced jumps.
It is not a disguised numerical check that the actual D happens to be positive.

Initially the tests included decreasing-set constraints as well. Those were
then removed: the saved dual certificates use only nonnegative coordinates
and the defects (2). This is the cleaner proposed lemma.

3. A SMALL EXACT SIGNED EXAMPLE, WITH AN EXPLICIT POSITIVE GROUPING

Take V={0,1,2,3}, sink dagger, p_z=1/2, and jump laws

    0 -> 3 or dagger, each with probability 1/2;
    1 -> 0, 2, 3 or dagger, each with probability 1/4;
    2 -> 3 or dagger, each with probability 1/2;
    3 -> 2 or dagger, each with probability 1/2.

Let target v=2, common A={0,1}, and extra source x=3. Write a(B)=a_2(B).
Exact elimination gives

    D_{0,1}(3;2)
      = a({1,3})/26 + 7 a({0,3})/240 + 2 a({0,1})/15
        - a({3})/1560.                                    (7)

This is a negative coefficient in the fully summed response, at a target
outside the common initial support.

Now force the common particle at 0 to jump when the common set is {0,3}.
With probability 1/2 it exits directly, leaving {3}. Otherwise it reaches 3,
where the surplus particle must jump to 2 or dagger, each with probability
1/2. The first alternative visits the target, while the second leaves {3}.
Consequently

    J_0 a({0,3}) = (3/4) a({3}),
    e_2({0,3},0) = a({0,3})-(3/4)a({3}) >= 0.              (8)

Substitution makes every term nonnegative:

    D_{0,1}(3;2)
      = a({1,3})/26 + 53 a({0,3})/1872 + 2 a({0,1})/15
        + [a({0,3})-(3/4)a({3})]/1170.                    (9)

The exact ordinary values are

    a({3})=3/4, a({1,3})=153/256,
    a({0,3})=21/32, a({0,1})=393/512.

A separate raw-instruction absorption calculation gives

    F_2(1_{0,1})=49/390,
    F_2(1_{0,1,3})=673/2496,
    D_{0,1}(3;2)=599/4160,

in exact agreement with both (7) and (9). The verification rebuilds the
24-edge signed system using Fraction arithmetic and independent raw-state
elimination. It does not rationalize saved floating-point matrices.

4. THE NEXT GENERAL LEMMA, AND ITS COUPLING INTERPRETATION

Proposed positive-flow lemma: for every admissible finite K and p, every
target v and support edge (A,x), the signed response admits (5).
This lemma remains UNPROVED. For p_z=0, the previous sleeper-elimination
formula divides by p_z; a general proof through that formula should first
treat p_z>0 and then justify the finite-system continuity limit.

There is a formulation that avoids assuming I-M invertible in general.
Seek a signed row w and nonnegative c,d satisfying

    w(I-M)=delta_(A,x),
    w G_v = c + sum_(B,z) d(B,z)(delta_B-J_z(B,. )).          (10)

Equations (2), (3), (10) would already prove the desired response positive.
The computations use w=delta_(A,x)(I-M)^(-1); they do not establish that
this inverse exists for every graph and parameter choice. Additional exact
relations among response edges can also be included if needed.

The flow interpretation of (5) is concrete. Regard the positive coefficients
of the signed row L as supply and its negative coefficients as demand. From
a support B one is allowed to redistribute an amount d(B,z) according to
the actual forced-jump kernel J_z(B,.). After all these redistributions the
remaining coefficient vector c must be nonnegative. A local move changes
the paired-history ledger by exactly the nonnegative event e_v(B,z).
Cycles and branching destinations are handled by flow balance. The jump law
cannot be chosen selectively after seeing a favorable destination.

This is a promising way to retain the relevant creation histories in grouped
form. The earlier state-only failure treated different avoidance coordinates
as nearly arbitrary; (2) records which alternate histories a forced jump
must produce. One may not simply discard the negative renewal branches.

A general construction of this flow is the main outstanding task. Even a
proof of (5) would first yield the occupation inequality and an exact ledger;
it would not automatically give the desired explicit, inverse-recognizable
stack-field transformation. A full coupling construction would still need to
realize the ledger on disjoint histories, or use an appropriate conditional
law construction. Neither of these is claimed here.

5. NUMERICAL EVIDENCE AND ITS LIMITS

The initial cone tests minimize each response over nonnegative avoidance
vectors that are decreasing and satisfy (2), normalized by a(empty)=1.
There were no violations beyond roundoff in 47 parameter/bound cases:

  - the four previous path/directed cases;
  - seven additional cases, including a five-site path with four common
    particles and directed kernels up to six sites;
  - 36 rational directed kernels on four to six sites, with heterogeneous
    sleep probabilities from {0.1,0.3,0.5,0.7,0.9} and up to three common
    particles. Seed and full kernels are saved.

The stronger dual tests seek coefficients in (5) using NO decreasing-set
constraints. They succeeded for all 4,364 response rows across the four
baseline cases and nine of the rational directed cases. Of those, 1,378
certificates use a positive forced-jump-defect coefficient. Maximum equality
residual was 1.39e-17.

Five further dual cases include five-site paths at p=0.01 and p=0.99,
six-site paths with up to four common particles, heterogeneous sleep rates,
and a directed five-site kernel. All 3,285 rows were feasible at solver
tolerance, with maximum residual 9.99e-10. These weaker residuals can hide
very small terms, so they should not be described as exact certificates.
The exact audit in Section 3 is independent and has zero rational residual.

Do not infer an arbitrary-graph result from these counts. Also do not infer
that D itself is superharmonic in its common-particle positions: that proposed
shortcut already fails numerically on the three-site path at p=1/2, with
common A={2}, extra x=1, target v=0, forcing the common particle at z=2.
The inequality (2) is for AVOIDANCE, not for the occupation response D.
