ARW MONOTONICITY: PARTICLE SWAPS AND TERMINATING CONTROLLED ROUTING
2026-10-06 UTC; continuation of AVOIDANCE_FLOW.txt

STATUS

The main add-one occupation conjecture and the general positive-flow lemma
remain open. This continuation makes the proposed flow more concrete:

  * its permitted moves are single-particle walks to a vacancy;
  * those moves have an explicit attractive one-discrepancy coupling;
  * every feasible positive-flow certificate has a causal randomized
    realization, with an explicit finite expected running-time bound;
  * the realization can be implemented by acceptable stack topplings, giving
    a nonnegative visitation-event representation of the signed response;
  * a small exact example rules out both coefficientwise positivity at a
    fixed particle count and the simplest uniform-routing formula.

The missing part is now the existence/construction of nonnegative flow
weights for the ARW response. Termination and the causal realization of a
given finite certificate need not remain separate conjectural steps.
This does NOT yet couple stabilization of the original two initial inputs.
The source mixture in the routing construction is determined by the signed
response coefficients, not by either original initial configuration.

1. THE FORCED-JUMP KERNEL IS A SINGLE MOVING PARTICLE

Use the notation of the preceding note. A is a binary common support,
v is the target, and J_z(A,B) is the forced-jump/IDLA kernel for z in A.

Remove the particle at z, hold one active particle at each site of A\{z},
and run a K-walk from z with a mandatory first step. Stop at the first vacant
site or the original sink. If it stops at y in V, the new support is
(A\{z}) union {y}; at the sink it is A\{z}. This is exactly J_z.
After a collision there is just one surplus particle to keep moving.
Sleep cards read with two active particles present have no effect.

For target-avoidance calculations, omit outcomes that hit v. Thus J_z is
substochastic on target-free supports, INCLUDING the empty support. Hitting
the original sink can remove a particle and is not the same as hitting v.

2. A RIGOROUS AUXILIARY PARTICLE-SWAP COUPLING

Consider the continuous-time process in which each occupied site z rings
at rate one and performs the update above. Couple supports A and A union {x}
using common site clocks and the same walk at common departures.

At a common departure z, remove z in both configurations and follow one walk.
Until it finds a vacancy in A\{z}, both walks coincide.

  * If the lower stopping site y is not x (including the sink), both stop
    there, the common support updates, and x remains the extra site.
  * If the lower walk stops at x, the upper walk continues through the
    occupied support (A\{z}) union {x}. Its eventual vacancy w is the new
    extra site. If the continuation exits, the two supports coalesce.

At an upper-only departure x, the moving extra particle walks through A
until a vacancy or the sink. This moves the discrepancy or removes it.

Every update preserves inclusion, so this is a genuine attractive process.
The same construction works with fixed site-dependent clock rates.
It is an auxiliary process, NOT ARW stabilization. In ARW the upper system
can wake a common particle which stays asleep below. A later upper-only
departure at that common occupied site need not preserve inclusion. The
auxiliary comparison does not by itself account for this sleep history.

Let R_support be minus the auxiliary generator, and R_edge minus the killed
generator on one-discrepancy states (A,x). The construction proves

    R_edge grad = grad R_support,
    (grad f)(A,x) = f(A union {x}) - f(A).                   (11)

R_edge has nonpositive off-diagonal entries and nonnegative row sums.
exclusion_edge_process.py constructs both matrices and the discrepancy
transitions. Numerical checks of (11) have residual below 9e-16; the coupling
argument above supplies the general justification.

3. A STRICT POTENTIAL FOR EVERY CONTROLLED ROUTING POLICY

Let h(z) be the expected number of K-walk steps from z to the original sink.
It is finite since the sink is accessible from every vertex, and satisfies

    h(z) = 1 + sum_w K(z,w) h(w),   h(sink)=0.

For a support A define Phi(A)=sum_{z in A} h(z), with Phi(empty)=0.
If a forced update from z stops at Y after tau steps, optional stopping gives

    Phi(A) - E[Phi((A\{z}) union {Y})] = E[tau] >= 1,       (12)

with the obvious deletion at the sink. On killing the routing procedure
when it hits v, assign potential zero to that cemetery state. Omitting the
nonnegative potential of the other remaining particles only increases the
left side of (12). Consequently

    (delta_A - J_z(A,.)) Phi >= 1                          (13)

on every nonempty target-free support and every occupied z.

This holds regardless of how the next occupied site is selected, including
randomized, history-dependent choices. A procedure that keeps making these
moves until it empties, hits v, or stops voluntarily therefore satisfies

    E_A[number of forced updates] <= Phi(A).               (14)

In particular it terminates almost surely. For any stationary policy pi,
the transition matrix J_pi restricted to nonempty target-free supports is
transient; I-J_pi is invertible and its inverse is nonnegative. No assumption
about the signed ARW matrix I-M is needed for this fact.

Equation (13) also excludes a nonzero nonnegative circulation:
if d>=0 and sum d(A,z)(delta_A-J_z(A,.)) is identically zero, multiplying by
Phi gives 0 >= sum d(A,z), forcing d=0. This is the useful no-hidden-cycles
statement for the proposed flow.

4. EVERY FEASIBLE CERTIFICATE HAS A CAUSAL REALIZATION

Fix one signed response row ell, indexed by target-free supports, with
ell(empty)=0. In the preceding renewal, ell is a row of L_v=(I-M)^(-1)G_v
when that inverse exists. More generally it can be wG_v for any w satisfying
w(I-M)=delta_(A,x), without assuming general invertibility.

Suppose the proposed lemma has supplied

    ell = c + sum_(B,z) d(B,z)(delta_B-J_z(B,.)), c,d>=0.   (15)

First all residual c on nonempty supports can be removed from the formula.
Choose any fixed stationary routing policy pi. On nonempty supports T,
write u=c_T (I-J_pi|_T)^(-1)>=0. Add u(B)pi(z|B) to d(B,z), and add the
resulting flow into empty to c(empty). This replaces (15) by

    ell = c0 delta_empty + sum_(B,z) d(B,z)(delta_B-J_z),
    c0>=0, d>=0.                                         (16)

This is an equivalent form of the certificate, not a stronger conjecture.
In particular, arbitrary nonempty residual coefficients are unnecessary.

Here is an explicit routing algorithm from (16). On nonempty supports put

    P(B)=max(ell(B),0),  N(B)=max(-ell(B),0),
    d_B=sum_z d(B,z),    W(B)=d_B+N(B).

Start with mass P(B) at B. At every visit to a state with W(B)>0:

  * stop there to pay its negative demand with probability N(B)/W(B);
  * choose the forced move z with probability d(B,z)/W(B), then sample
    its destination according to the actual J_z(B,.).

The missing J mass is a target hit. Reaching empty is another terminal
outcome. A state with W=0 receives no source or incoming mass and needs no
rule. The local balance equation is

    W(B) = P(B) + sum_(C,z) d(C,z) J_z(C,B).               (17)

By transience, its unique finite solution is the actual expected arrival
mass. Hence demand B receives exactly N(B), empty receives exactly c0,
and every routing step uses the original jump probabilities. This turns
the finite algebraic certificate into an executable, causal procedure.
It never spends negative mass or assumes funding from an inaccessible cycle.

There is also a quantitative audit:

    sum_(B,z) d(B,z) <= ell Phi <= sum_B P(B) Phi(B).      (18)

The first inequality follows from (13) and (16); the second from N>=0.

For the actual avoidance vector a_v, the route has a stack realization.
Choose its source B with probability P(B)/m, m=sum P. At each forced update,
topple through the first jump acceptably, then legally move the surplus
until vacant. All remaining suffixes are fresh. Make the stop/action choice
using independent auxiliary randomness and the exposed support only. On
termination, finish ordinary stabilization (also after a target hit).
The entire modified execution is acceptable and stabilizing. Least action
implies that its visited set contains the original legal execution's visited
set. Let I_original and I_routed be their target-avoidance indicators. Then

    I_original >= I_routed,
    m E[I_original] = sum_B P(B)a_v(B),
    m E[I_routed] = sum_B N(B)a_v(B) + c0.

Consequently the desired response, conditional on (16), has the identity

    D = ell a_v
      = c0 + m P(I_original=1, I_routed=0) >= 0.           (19)

If m=0 the certificate is zero and no routing is needed. Formula (19) is
a genuine nonnegative event representation for a certified row. It is not
an inverse-recognizable transformation between the original lower and upper
ARW stack histories. The source mixture P comes from ell.

5. AN EXACT SMALL OBSTRUCTION AND AN EXACT REPAIR

Take vertices 0,1,2,3, with the following jump laws:

    0 -> 2 with probability 1;
    1 -> 0 or sink, each probability 1/2;
    2 -> 3 or sink, each probability 1/2;
    3 -> 0 or 1, each probability 1/2.

Let p=(1/16,1/2,1/2,1/16), common A={1,3}, extra x=2, target v=1.
Rebuild the entire 24-edge renewal with at most two common particles using
exact Fraction arithmetic. In its response row, the coefficient of a_v({0,2})
is strictly negative, approximately -0.00941462267. Since |{0,2}|=|A|=2,
one cannot group every row by only moving mass to smaller particle counts.
Redistribution at the same particle count is essential in this formulation.

For fixed site rates r_z, define on nonempty target-free supports

    R_r(B,.) = sum_(z in B) r_z (delta_B-J_z(B,.)),
    U_r = ell_T R_r^(-1).                                 (20)

If U_r>=0, then d(B,z)=U_r(B)r_z gives (16), with the empty coefficient
determined by the outgoing empty flux. The inverse exists by Section 3.

For uniform r_z=1 in this example, U_r({0,2}) is strictly negative,
approximately -0.00101936137. Thus a uniform Green-function formula cannot
prove the heterogeneous theorem. Taking r=(15,1,1,15)=(1-p)/p repairs this
particular row: every U_r coefficient is nonnegative. The exact audit checks
the row equality, the causal route's transition probabilities, its full
expected arrival masses, every paid demand, and the empty flux. It also
checks the strict potential bound and verifies D independently through raw
ARW absorption. The exact rational coefficients are in the JSON certificate.

Do not promote r=(1-p)/p to a general answer: a different six-site directed
example already gives a numerical coefficient near -1.99e-6 for that rule
(sleep_rate_flow_probe_results.json). Flexible action weights still pass the
cone check in the tested cases. The necessary rule may depend on the current
support and the response row; existence of one such rule is what is missing.

6. COMPUTATION, LIMITS, AND THE NEXT PROOF TARGET

edge_renewal.py builds the same signed matrix directly on binary edges.
It batches trapped stabilization laws, evaluates IDLA by successive one-
particle walks, and avoids dense matrices on all particle multiplicities.
Its four baseline matrices agree with the old implementation to 1.4e-16;
forced-jump kernels agree to 1.2e-16. This makes the full seven-site support
hierarchy practical (about ten seconds in this environment).

New cone tests used only a>=0 and J_z a<=a, normalized by sum a=1.
There were no violations beyond solver tolerance in 18 more recurrent
four/five-site cases or in six full-support six/seven-site cases. The latter
include homogeneous and heterogeneous sleep rates and a single sink exit.
These numerical tests are evidence for (15), not a proof. Same-particle-count
negative coefficients also occur in the homogeneous larger examples.

Uniform-rate U in (20) remained nonnegative up to roundoff in all 18 saved
additional homogeneous cases and in the six larger full-support cases
(including their heterogeneous members). This is a possible simpler
homogeneous sub-lemma, still unproved. Neither this observation nor the
attractive auxiliary chain establishes ARW occupation monotonicity.

Other diagnostics: individual M^k G_v rows for k=1,...,4 pass the cone test
in the exact obstruction example (baseline checks also passed). Square
identities exchanging which particle is called extra do not suffice to make
the signed M rows coefficientwise nonnegative in all tested cases. Applying
the natural auxiliary edge Laplacian directly to the uniform Green weights
also leaves negative forcing terms. These are failed shortcuts, not proofs
against a more elaborate use of the particle-swap chain.

The next general proof target can now be stated without a termination gap:

    construct w, d>=0, c0>=0 with
    w(I-M)=delta_(A,x),
    wG_v=c0 delta_empty + sum d(B,z)(delta_B-J_z(B,.)).     (21)

One possible route is to trace an unmatched sleep history backwards and
transfer the distinguished extra particle when a common walk reaches its
site, as in Section 2. The missing invariant must show that the associated
weights satisfy the balance in (21) without becoming negative. Once those
weights exist, Sections 3-4 provide the terminating realization. No such
general invariant is claimed in this checkpoint.
