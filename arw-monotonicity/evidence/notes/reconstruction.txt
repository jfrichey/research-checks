ARW BACKWARD COUPLING: DELAYED RELEASE AND FIRST-CONTACT RENEWAL
2026-10-06, Shanghai time

Status: the general single-site add-one conjecture is still open here.
This continuation follows BUFFER_SWITCHING.txt. It constructs an exact,
legal comparison coupling using input records and one delayed packet. That
coupling is not monotone: its remaining bad outcomes still require a
history-aware cancellation. A renewal decomposition identifies where that
cancellation must occur. It closes rigorously with one common particle.

The most useful conclusions for the next pass are:

  * One potential arrow per arrival makes used/unread changes canonical.
    The extra initial sleeper can be released AFTER the entire common
    history, as one fresh avalanche. Both marginal laws are exact.
  * The general response is D = T D + g + r. T is nonnegative, strictly
    substochastic; g is nonnegative; r consists of reachable mixed-state
    first contacts, retaining the histories that created their sleepers.
  * Individual mixed contacts really can have negative response. The
    surrounding renewal terms can compensate them exactly.
  * Algebraically removing sleepers, then applying IDLA and source
    harmonicity, does NOT always yield a nonnegative transition kernel.
    An exact surviving coefficient is -8/2025 on a homogeneous four-site
    path. Mere monotonicity of avoidance probabilities is also insufficient
    for the resulting state-only linear system.

These are statements about particular reconstruction mechanisms. None of
the negative coefficients or conditional inventories is a counterexample
to the main ARW conjecture. No historical novelty claim is being made for
the standard Abelian/strong-Markov facts used below.

1. A UNIFORM INPUT RECORD WITH AN UNREAD POTENTIAL ARROW

At site z, preassign independent records

    (B_z(m), D_z(m)), m=1,2,...,

where B_z(m) is Bernoulli(p_z), and D_z(m) has jump law K(z,.).
An initial sleeper, if present, has one further independent arrow D_z(0).
Its initial occupancy is fixed, not an additional Bernoulli trial.

When input m arrives:

  - If the previous resident exists, emit its waiting arrow D_z(m-1).
    For a never-visited initial sleeper this means D_z(0).
  - Read B_z(m). If it is 0, emit D_z(m) immediately. If it is 1,
    retain the incoming particle and leave D_z(m) waiting.

For initial buffer b=0, the emitted arrow word after m inputs is
D_z(1),...,D_z(m-B_z(m)). For b=1 it is
D_z(0),...,D_z(m-B_z(m)), when m>=1. With m=0 there are no outputs.
The lengths are m+b-B_z(m), as in the previous buffer construction.
Output words grow by prefixes, and the processor is Abelian.

All visited input records have the same product form, even if the final
record's arrow is unread. This is an augmentation by independent variables,
not an assumption that an adaptively selected jump has its original law.

Fixed terminal-arrow padding is still insufficient for a general repair.
On the path dagger-0-1-2-dagger, take common eta=delta_0, extra initial
sleeper at 1, target 0. A bad pair can be:

  lower: sleep at 0; its unused arrow is 0->dagger.
  upper: jump 0->1; reset 1 to occupied, releasing the initial resident
         along 1->2; reset 2 to empty and jump 2->dagger. The terminal
         resident at 1 has unused arrow 1->2.

The combined reset outcomes are (1,0) at 0, (1) at 1, and (0) at 2.
The combined potential arrows are 0->1, 0->dagger, two 1->2 arrows,
and 2->dagger. No good pair has this padded inventory: the good upper
must sleep at 0 and never wake its initial resident at 1; the lower must
go 0->1 and sleep. Neither copy then processes an input at 2.

Thus a complete reset-and-arrow record at 2 becomes unused. A longer
downstream path, or a sequence of self-loops, makes the discarded block
arbitrarily long. The natural reconstruction needs whole unused blocks,
not just the terminal arrow. padded_record_pools.py exhaustively confirms
the first obstruction: four combined resets, one bad pair, zero good pairs
in that fixed padded inventory. This is not a truncation artifact.

2. A PATHWISE DELAYED-RELEASE COMPARISON COUPLING

Start the lower system from all-active eta, with empty buffers. In the
upper system add an initial sleeper at y outside supp(eta). Give both
systems the same input records (B_z(m),D_z(m)), m>=1. Give the upper's
initial sleeper its independent D_y(0). Each copy individually has the
correct independent book law.

Process the common inputs as in the lower system. When the upper initial
sleeper is first awakened, emit its extra packet but hold its delivery.
Every subsequent common input record is identical in both copies. After
the common inputs finish, there are precisely two possibilities:

  (a) y was never visited. The upper has the lower final configuration
      plus the untouched sleeper at y.
  (b) y was visited. Both buffer configurations now equal the lower final
      configuration sigma. The upper additionally has the held packet,
      whose destination is D_y(0), independent with law K(y,.).

In (b), deliver that packet and stabilize using the fresh suffixes of the
input records. If its destination is the sink, there is nothing to do.

For fixed records this delayed schedule has the same final configuration,
input counts, and output counts as ordinary delivery in the upper system.
Legality is in the Abelian pending-input network: an emitted packet may
wait before delivery. It is not a claim that a physically present active
particle can be ignored in a raw sleep decision. The equivalent raw-stack
proof follows in Section 3.

This solves a legal reconstruction and marginal-law problem for the
comparison coupling. It does NOT repair all bad target outcomes. The saved
sampler includes a bad outcome to make that limitation explicit.

Let V_eta be the visited set of the common history and sigma its final
stable configuration. Write f_v(sigma)=1{sigma_v is occupied}. For a stable
configuration let A_z be addition of one active particle at z followed by
stabilization, and A_dagger the identity. Define P_y=sum_z K(y,z) A_z.
Then the exact sleeper-insertion response is

  B_eta(y;v)
    = 1{y=v} P_eta(y notin V_eta)
      + E_eta[1{y in V_eta} (P_y f_v(sigma)-f_v(sigma))].       (1)

There is also an exact FULL-LAW version: on the no-visit branch add the
untouched sleeper; on the visit branch apply P_y to the visited-history
terminal subprobability measure. Thus a potentially large deleted or added
history is represented by one normalized fresh-avalanche kernel.

Equation (1) also holds with other common initial sleepers, provided y is
initially vacant. The all-active hypothesis becomes important for positivity,
not for this identity.

3. FIRST-CONTACT STOPPING LINES AND THE GENERAL RENEWAL EQUATION

The previous first-sleep reduction gives a substochastic kernel H_eta(x,y):
an extra walker from x sleeps with probability p_z only outside supp(eta),
and stops on that sleep or at the sink. Its untouched common particles stay
all active. For a selected sleep site y, now run the common ARW from eta
until its first arrival at y, stopping BEFORE a toppling at y. If there is
no such arrival, run it to stabilization. This is an ordinary raw-stack
stopping line.

On a first arrival, call the common configuration C_y. It has one active
particle at y. The upper configuration is exactly C_y+delta_y, with two
active particles at y. The unused stacks are fresh. Let

    Delta_v(C,y)=F_v(C+delta_y)-F_v(C),

where C is now allowed to contain sleepers away from its active site y.
The complete response identity is

  D_eta(x;v) = sum_y H_eta(x,y) [
      1{y=v} P_eta(no arrival at y)
      + E_eta[1{arrival at y} Delta_v(C_y,y)] ].                (2)

This supplies another proof of (1). At the first-contact configuration C,
the extra particle is at an already active site y. Its first forced jump
leaves C intact and adds one active particle at a K(y,.) destination.
By Abelianity, that addition can be postponed until after stabilizing C.

For a fixed bound N on the number of common particles, index the all-active
states s=(eta,x) with 1<=|eta|<=N. Split first contacts into all-active C_y
and mixed C_y. Put their respective contributions into T and r, and put
the no-contact positive contribution into g. Simultaneously for all targets,

    D = T D + g + r,                                         (3)
    D = (I-T)^(-1)(g+r) = sum_(k>=0) T^k(g+r).                (4)

T is nonnegative. Its row sum is at most the probability that the extra
walker sleeps before exiting. This is strictly less than one, since the
sink is accessible and every p_z<1. There are finitely many indexed states,
so the maximum row sum is <1. The renewal expansion converges absolutely.
The vector g is nonnegative. The vector r need not be nonnegative.

A useful equivalent stopping line freezes every common arrival at y and
fully stabilizes V\{y}. Then C_y consists of k active particles at y and
sleepers elsewhere. This changes T and r while preserving their total
response. It does not make mixed-state signs disappear.

4. THE RENEWAL CLOSES WITH ONE COMMON PARTICLE

This is a rigorous arbitrary-finite-graph result, not just a graph enumeration.
With one common particle there can be no other sleeper at its first contact
with y. Thus C_y=delta_y and r=0.

Let h_a(y) be the probability that one ARW particle started at a visits y
before sleeping or exiting. Let H_a(a,y) mean H_(delta_a)(a,y). Set

    d_a(v)=F_v(2 delta_a)-F_v(delta_a),
    T(a,y)=H_a(a,y) h_a(y),
    g_a(v)=H_a(a,v) [1-h_a(v)].

The diagonal column H_a(a,a) is zero. Then

    d(v)=T d(v)+g(v),
    d(v)=sum_(k>=0) T^k g(v) >= 0.                           (5)

For a general added active source x, the response from delta_a is

  D_(delta_a)(x;v)
   = sum_y H_a(x,y) [h_a(y)d_y(v)+1{y=v}(1-h_a(y))] >= 0.    (6)

An initially sleeping extra at y has the bracketed response directly.
For computation, with Q(z,w)=(1-p_z)K(z,w) and G=(I-Q)^(-1),
one has h_a(y)=G(a,y)/G(y,y).

There is also a coupling proving FULL occupied-set inclusion in this
one-common-particle case. When both upper particles are active at a,
freeze the common particle and move the extra to its first sleep outside
{a} or to the sink. If it exits, couple the remaining common walk exactly.
If it sleeps at y, move the common walk until it sleeps, exits, or hits y.
On sleep/exit before hitting y, the extra sleeper remains and the terminal
sets are ordered. On hitting y, the systems are again one versus two active
particles at the same site y, with fresh suffixes. Repeat. The substochastic
kernel T gives almost-sure termination. The same first stage handles an
extra active particle initially at any x.

The all-active condition matters in extending this argument: with several
common particles, one may have fallen asleep before another reaches y.

5. A REACHABLE NEGATIVE MIXED BOUNDARY, WITH EXACT COMPENSATION

Use the three-site path dagger-0-1-2-dagger and p=1/2. Let common
eta=(1,0,1), added active source x=0, and target v=0. Use the lowest-active
site schedule for the first-contact common history.

The extra's first sleep is at 1 with probability 1/3. The common first
contact at 1 has C=(-1,1,0) with probability 1/8: the particle at 0 sleeps,
and the particle at 2 jumps to 1. Consequently this mixed branch has
unconditional weight 1/24. Exact absorbing-chain calculation gives

    Delta_0((-1,1,0),1) = -305/1722.

Its contribution is -305/41328. The all-active renewal branches contribute
1061/41328. Their sum is

    D_(1,0,1)(0;0) = 3/164 > 0.                              (7)

The common all-active contact states and weights are (0,1,1) with weight
1/12, and (0,1,0) with weight 1/48. This shows concretely why a reconstruction
cannot demand nonnegative response at each contact. Compensation can occur
across the renewal history. verify_reconstruction_exact.py checks (7) with
Fraction arithmetic independently of the sparse floating-point solver.

6. UNREADING A SLEEP: AN EXACT ALGEBRAIC RULE AND ITS LIMIT

Suppose C_z is sleeping, C_y is active, and z!=y. Let C^0 remove the
sleeper at z. For p_z>0, conditioning the first instruction of an active
particle placed at z gives

  Delta_v(C,y)
    = (1/p_z) Delta_v(C^0+delta_z,y)
      - ((1-p_z)/p_z) sum_w K(z,w) Delta_v(C^0+delta_w,y).     (8)

An active addition at another sleeping site wakes it in this formula; a
sink destination adds nothing. Recursively applying (8) removes all initial
sleepers. The factor p_z in the history that created a sleeper can cancel
the denominator 1/p_z. The negative jump alternatives must still be paired
with other histories; they cannot be discarded.

This gives r=R D for an explicit signed matrix R, so

    D=(T+R)D+g.                                             (9)

On the three-site example, T+R has a coefficient -1/48 from ((1,0,1),0)
to ((0,2,0),1). It is not itself a nonnegative transition matrix.

Some negatives cancel after a legitimate extra reduction. First move all
surplus common particles by IDLA, keeping one active particle at every
occupied site. This expresses each D_eta(x) as a nonnegative mixture of
responses with binary common support. Then use source harmonicity

    D_A(x)=sum_z K(x,z)D_A(z), x in A,

to express occupied-source responses through sources outside A.
These operations preserve the real response and reduce the unknowns to
Boolean-lattice edges (A,x), x notin A.

For example, D_(0,2,0)(1) is one half each of D_(1,1,0)(1) and
D_(0,1,1)(1). In the three-site row above this turns the apparent negative
term into the positive identity

  D_(1,0,1)(0;v)
    = (7/96)[D_(1,1,0)(1;v)+D_(0,1,1)(1;v)]
      + (3/16)1{v=1}.                                      (10)

However, the same reductions fail to make all coefficients nonnegative
in general, even on a homogeneous path. On dagger-0-1-2-3-dagger, p=1/2,
the reduced equation from common A={0,3}, extra x=1 has coefficient

    -8/2025                                                (11)

on the edge response with common A'={1,2}, extra x'=0.

An exact small certificate for (11): the first-sleep weights at 1 and 2
are 28/45 and 8/45. Freeze common arrivals at the selected site. After (8),
IDLA reduction, and source harmonicity, the corresponding coefficients
are -1/180 and -1/360. Their weighted sum is -8/2025. For frozen site 1,
the particle from 3 hits 1, sleeps at 2, sleeps at 3, or exits with
probabilities 1/15, 2/15, 8/15, 4/15. The site-2 calculation is reflected.
All these numbers are checked with rational arithmetic in the exact audit.

This rules out the PARTICULAR shortcut of interpreting this fully reduced
state matrix as a Markov renewal kernel. It does not rule out additional
history switches, different normal forms, or cancellations between edges.

7. AVOIDANCE MONOTONICITY ALONE DOES NOT REPAIR THAT SHORTCUT

For binary common support A, the direct reward at target v is

    g_(A,x)(v)=H_A(x,v) a_A(v),
    a_A(v)=P_A(no particle ever visits v).

Same-stack odometer monotonicity implies a_A(v) decreases as A grows.
Could this restriction, without retaining more history, force the reduced
signed system to have a nonnegative answer? The saved cone test says no.
It substitutes all 0/1 decreasing functions on subsets of V\{v}, the
extreme layer sets of the cone of nonnegative decreasing functions.
Some produce a negative response in the reduced system, including on the
homogeneous four-site path. These artificial avoidance functions are NOT
claimed to be realizable by ARW. The conclusion is only that monotonicity
of a_A(v), by itself, is too weak. Actual creation and avoidance histories
carry additional constraints that the state-only reduction has discarded.

8. THE NEXT GENERAL COUPLING TARGET

The new useful object is a common completed history, its visited set, and
one delayed extra avalanche. All consumed-to-unused changes can be stored
as complete input-record blocks; their unconditioned continuation has total
mass one. First-contact renewal supplies finite stopping-line boundaries
and a summable expansion on every finite graph.

The remaining target is a weight-preserving cancellation of BAD mixed
boundary blocks with GOOD blocks across this renewal expansion, retaining
the earlier records that created the common sleepers. Equation (8) supplies
the exact local alternative: reinstate the active predecessor, and account
for its possible jump histories. The example (10) shows that reinserting
and legally moving a surplus particle can provide real compensation.
Equation (11) shows that this cannot be reduced to independently positive
coefficients on final configurations or simple support edges.

A successful backward rule still needs a recognizable inverse or an exact
disjoint fractional ledger, and a termination/zero-residual argument. Merely
putting discarded records in an unused continuation does not establish those
claims. In particular, the delayed-release coupling itself has bad target
outcomes, and is not the desired final monotone coupling.

9. IMPLEMENTATIONS AND CHECKS

delayed_release_sampler.py
  Input-record books and the explicit delayed-packet reconstruction.
  600 fixed-field comparisons against ordinary FIFO and LIFO upper execution
  agree in final bits, arrival counts, departure counts, and conservation.
  357 histories awaken the extra sleeper; 243 leave it untouched.
  One nonmonotone sample is retained as an explicit limitation.

marked_reconstruction.py
  Raw-instruction first-contact laws, full-law delayed-release identity,
  the one-common-particle renewal, and general signed renewal reductions.
  31 full-law checks include directed self-loops, unequal sleep probabilities,
  repeated common particles, and mixed common initial backgrounds.
  Maximum delayed-law error: 2.23e-16.
  Maximum one-common-particle response error: 1.77e-16.
  The numerical checks corroborate the derivations; they are not proofs of
  the general conjecture.

verify_reconstruction_exact.py
  Independent Fraction elimination for (7), plus the rational coefficient
  certificate (11). Results: reconstruction_exact_verification.json.

padded_record_pools.py
  Complete finite history enumeration with terminal arrows included.
  Results: padded_record_pool_results.json and
  padded_record_pool_varied_results.json. The three-site four-reset
  obstruction excludes all good histories of that fixed padded inventory.

reconstruction_probes.py
  Reproduces the state-matrix and avoidance-cone failures on three- and
  four-site paths and a directed kernel. Results retain the matrices and
  parameters in reconstruction_probe_results.json. First-contact and fully
  trapped versions give the same closed matrix in these checked examples;
  no general schedule-invariance theorem for that matrix is asserted here.

Run each Python script from this research directory. The principal solvers
use NumPy and SciPy; the delayed sampler and exact audit use the standard
library. The previous continuation files and their certificates remain
unchanged.
